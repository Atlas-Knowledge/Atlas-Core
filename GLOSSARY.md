# Atlas Glossary

> **The canonical vocabulary of the Atlas ecosystem.**

---

# Purpose

This glossary defines the official terminology used throughout the Atlas ecosystem.

Every Atlas specification, repository, RFC, ADR, implementation, and document should use these definitions consistently.

If a conflict exists between another document and this glossary, this glossary is the canonical reference.

---

# Core Concepts

## Atlas

The complete standards-driven ecosystem for building trustworthy knowledge infrastructure.

---

## Atlas Core

The Program Root Repository responsible for governance, architecture, ecosystem coordination, and canonical program documentation.

---

## Atlas Foundation

The repository responsible for community governance, contribution policies, security policies, and organizational management.

---

## Atlas Standards

The canonical repository containing normative specifications that define Atlas standards.

---

## Atlas Repository Model (ARM)

The official model defining the structure, governance, lifecycle, and organization of every Atlas repository.

---

## Repository

An independently versioned component within the Atlas ecosystem with a clearly defined responsibility.

---

## Ecosystem

The complete collection of repositories, standards, governance, tooling, and reference implementations that together form Atlas.

---

## Program

The complete Atlas initiative composed of multiple coordinated repositories.

---

## Architecture

The high-level structural design governing relationships between Atlas repositories and ecosystem components.

---

## Governance

The processes, policies, roles, and decision-making mechanisms that guide the evolution of Atlas.

---

## Standard

A normative specification defining expected behavior.

Standards are implementation-independent.

---

## Specification

A formal technical definition describing requirements, behavior, constraints, or interfaces.

---

## RFC (Request for Comments)

A proposal describing a significant new capability, standard, or architectural change under community review.

---

## ADR (Architecture Decision Record)

A permanent historical record documenting an accepted architectural decision.

---

## Conformance

The process of verifying that an implementation satisfies a published standard.

---

## Conformance Test Suite (CTS)

A collection of tests used to verify that implementations conform to Atlas standards.

---

## Reference Implementation

An implementation demonstrating how a standard can be correctly realized.

Reference implementations are examples of compliance, not the standard itself.

---

## Canonical Source

The single authoritative source for a specific category of information.

Every category has exactly one canonical source.

---

## Compatibility Matrix

A document defining supported compatibility relationships between independent Atlas components.

---

## Semantic Versioning

The versioning scheme used throughout Atlas repositories.

---

## Technology Neutrality

The principle that Atlas standards do not prescribe specific programming languages, frameworks, vendors, or cloud providers.

---

## Interoperability

The ability of independent systems to communicate and work together through shared standards.

---

## Knowledge

Information that has been organized, validated, and connected in a meaningful way.

---

## Evidence

Verifiable information supporting a claim or conclusion.

---

## Discovery

The creation of new understanding through validated knowledge and evidence.

---

## Transparency

The ability to understand, inspect, and verify processes, decisions, and reasoning.

---

## Sustainability

The ability of the Atlas ecosystem to evolve responsibly over long periods while maintaining stability and interoperability.

---

## Repository Lifecycle

The standard lifecycle followed by every Atlas repository.

```
Proposed

↓

Planned

↓

Development

↓

Review

↓

Stable

↓

Maintenance

↓

Deprecated

↓

Archived
```

---

# Naming Conventions

Official Atlas repositories use the following naming pattern:

```
Atlas-<Component>
```

Examples:

- Atlas-Core
- Atlas-Standards
- Atlas-Kernel
- Atlas-SDK

---

# Terminology Rules

All Atlas documentation shall:

- Use glossary terms consistently.
- Avoid ambiguous terminology.
- Prefer official Atlas vocabulary.
- Update this glossary before introducing new ecosystem terminology.

---

# Glossary Governance

Changes to glossary definitions require:

- RFC (when introducing or modifying core terminology)
- Review
- Approval

This document is the canonical source of terminology for the Atlas ecosystem.

Shared language enables shared understanding.

A consistent vocabulary is essential for building interoperable systems, sustainable standards, and trustworthy knowledge infrastructure.
